# Product opportunities roadmap

> These are future proposals, not current product capabilities. Comment automation currently sends public replies only; DM-related items require separate product authorization, platform-policy review, and permission verification before implementation.

This is a research backlog, not a promise or a statement that competitors lack these capabilities. Priorities are hypotheses for customer validation. Complexity is relative because estimates require implementation discovery.

| Priority | Opportunity and customer problem | Evidence / confidence | Complexity | Dependencies | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Automation failure alerts.** Merchants discover a disconnected account, lost permission, queue backlog, or repeated send failure only after customers complain. | Respond.io users have requested AI failure notifications ([request](https://roadmap.respond.io/feature-request/p/ai-agent-notify-the-team-when-an-ai-agent-fails)); direct relevance is credible, local demand still needs validation. | Medium | Existing notification delivery, deduplication window, actionable failure taxonomy and owner preferences. | Interview active merchants; alert on synthetic repeated failures and verify one deduplicated, actionable notification per incident. |
| P1 | **Knowledge-source transparency and freshness.** Merchants need to verify which connected source supplied a customer-facing fact and spot stale or conflicting information. | Intercom's public Evals/Releases materials show an industry focus on validating AI changes ([overview](https://www.intercom.com/blog/announcing-evals-and-releases)); source attribution demand is a product hypothesis. | High | Source provenance in retrieval results, freshness metadata, safe answer audit records; never expose chain-of-thought. | User study with support teams; compare attribution accuracy to source records and measure stale-data detection. |
| P1 | **Account-aware automation testing.** A rule can behave differently across connected Pages/accounts or store catalogs. | Naz's multi-account model makes this a concrete integration risk; competitor parity and demand remain unverified. | Medium | Shared resolver, account-scoped fixtures, simulator and regression scenarios. | Run the same scenario against two authorized test accounts and verify outputs never cross account boundaries. |
| P1 | **Recovery for failed actions.** Operators need an idempotent way to retry a known transient failure without duplicating a successful send. | Failure-notification requests support the discoverability pain; safe recovery is an engineering hypothesis. | High | Durable per-action status, provider idempotency behavior, authorization and audit trail. | Inject timeout/partial-success cases; retry and prove one external action at most. |
| P2 | **Story-reply triggers.** Social sellers want to act on story responses without monitoring every account manually. | Manychat documents Instagram comment triggers, demonstrating category usage rather than unmet demand ([comments trigger](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/14281316989724-Instagram-Post-and-Reel-Comments-trigger)); story capability requires Meta API verification. | Medium | Current official Meta event support, permissions, account eligibility and new event normalization. | Verify official platform documentation and app permissions; pilot with synthetic/webhook fixtures before production enablement. |
| P2 | **DM-keyword triggers.** Trigger a workflow when an inbound message includes configured intent terms. | Common automation pattern; no claim of competitor gap. | Medium | Existing inbound message pipeline, consent rules, deduplication and conversation assignment. | Test exact/contains/excluded keywords across supported channels, including duplicate deliveries. |
| P2 | **Comment spam hiding.** Reduce public spam without hiding legitimate customer feedback. | Manychat troubleshooting documents account and automation limitations, not demand for moderation ([troubleshooting](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/14281308423452-Instagram-automation-troubleshooting)); demand remains unverified. | High | Platform moderation API availability, reversible moderation, audit and false-positive safeguards. | Shadow-classify comments first; compare precision/recall with moderator decisions before enabling hiding. |
| P2 | **Lead capture with explicit consent.** Collect permitted contact details and consent before sending a link or follow-up. | A plausible social-commerce workflow; no strong direct customer evidence found in this audit. | High | Consent wording/storage, privacy retention, channel messaging eligibility, unsubscribe controls. | Merchant interviews and legal review; measure opt-in completion and complaint rate in a controlled test. |
| P2 | **Comment-to-DM conversion analytics.** Connect comments, permitted follow-up, leads and orders where provider IDs permit reliable attribution. | Respond.io request board and comment automation tooling show adjacent workflow interest; cross-platform attribution remains uncertain. | High | Stable event relationships, permitted attribution identifiers, privacy-safe analytics. | Validate attribution against known test journeys and expose an explicit unattributed category. |
| P2 | **Scheduled public replies with approval.** Prepare replies for later publication while retaining human review. | Scheduling is common in social tools; automation-specific need has not been confirmed. | Medium | Queue cancellation, approval states, current comment eligibility and account health checks. | Measure approval time and cancellation rate with synthetic comments before enabling live sending. |
| P2 | **Lead export.** Let authorized users download captured leads for existing CRM workflows. | Requested in the supplied product backlog; no external demand evidence established. | Medium | Consent record, export permissions, audit logging, rate limits and data minimization. | Verify exported rows against source records, permissions, and deletion/retention rules. |
| P2 | **Message reaction and event triggers.** Use permitted reactions as workflow inputs. | Research request only; platform webhook coverage and permissions must be confirmed per provider. | High | Official API event support, normalization, deduplication, tenant-safe routing. | Verify against current official platform docs, then run webhook fixtures for each supported event. |
| P3 | **Configuration import/export.** Reuse bot and rule settings across authorized workspaces without copying secrets. | Operational hypothesis for agencies and multi-store merchants. | Medium | Versioned schema, secret exclusion, tenant authorization, migration/compatibility handling. | Import/export sanitized configuration between isolated test tenants and verify credentials are omitted. |
| P3 | **Usage and cost visibility.** Explain automation volume and provider/model cost drivers before bills become surprising. | User concern about affordable alternatives appears in community discussions, but sample size is limited ([discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/automation/comments/1nfcf80/is_there_any_free_manychat_alternative/)). | Medium | Accurate usage ledger, provider cost inputs and plan entitlements. | Reconcile dashboard totals to provider invoices and merchant expectations. |
| P3 | **More uniform ecommerce feature parity.** Merchants need predictable product, order, and inventory actions across providers. | Gorgias explicitly documents provider-specific feature compatibility ([compatibility matrix](https://docs.gorgias.com/en-US/compatibility-of-automation-features-with-ecommerce-store-providers-1749413)); uniform parity is not always technically available. | High | Provider capability matrix, scoped adapters, tests per connection/provider. | Publish supported actions by provider and verify each with isolated provider test accounts. |
| P3 | **Knowledge-gap report.** Identify unanswered or escalated questions so merchants can improve source material. | Intercom's evaluations work supports testing as a category; a gap report is an unvalidated extension. | Medium | Safe classification of actual events, privacy controls, source links and feedback loop. | Compare suggested gaps with merchant review and track whether documented answers reduce repeat escalations. |

## Research notes

- A Respond.io feature request for automated AI-agent testing exists and showed community interest during this audit; that is evidence of a requested workflow, not proof of market-wide unmet need.
- Intercom's Evals/Releases materials establish that evaluation and controlled AI changes are already recognized product patterns. Naz's opportunity is to connect account-specific tests to actual comment decisions and outcomes, not to claim testing itself is unique.
- Manychat documents Instagram post/reel comment automation. Its troubleshooting guide discusses operational limitations. This confirms category maturity and the need for clear diagnostics, not a competitor defect.
- Public community discussions about alternatives and cost are anecdotal. They should guide interviews, not serve as quantified demand.
- Verify platform permissions and policy from current official provider documentation before implementing any message action. Do not infer that a webhook or permission exists from a third-party product feature.
